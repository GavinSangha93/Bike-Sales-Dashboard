# Bike Sales Dashboard

An Excel analysis of customer demographics and purchasing behavior, presented through an interactive dashboard built with PivotTables, PivotCharts, and slicers.

## Business Question

Which customer characteristics are associated with bike purchases, and how could a retailer use those patterns to improve audience segmentation and marketing decisions?

## What I Built

- Reviewed and prepared the source data for analysis.
- Standardized categorical values and created age brackets.
- Built PivotTables to compare purchases across income, age, commute distance, and other customer attributes.
- Created PivotCharts and slicers for interactive exploration.
- Combined the analysis into a single Excel dashboard.

## Dataset

The dataset contains customer-level attributes including marital status, gender, income, children, education, occupation, home ownership, cars owned, commute distance, region, age, and purchase status.

## Key Findings

- Customers who purchased bikes had a higher average income in the displayed comparison.
- Middle-aged customers represented the largest purchasing segment.
- Purchase activity varied across commute-distance categories.
- The interactive filters allow the same patterns to be compared across demographic segments.

These findings describe associations in this dataset and do not establish that any individual characteristic causes a purchase.

## Business Use

A retailer could use this dashboard as an exploratory segmentation tool when deciding which audiences warrant further research or campaign testing. Any real targeting decision should be validated with current customer data, conversion rates, and campaign results.

## Limitations and Next Steps

- The analysis is descriptive and does not measure causation.
- The dataset does not include campaign cost, conversion value, or customer lifetime value.
- Category-level counts may be affected by the composition of the sample.
- A next step would be to calculate purchase rates within each segment rather than relying only on raw counts and averages.
